# Collecting messages

## The request (adapt to the channel and language)

**Work (Slack, Teams, email):**
> We're putting together a card for **Meena**, whose last day is **Friday**.
> In 1–3 sentences, share **one moment with her you remember** and **what
> difference she made** for you or the team. Reply here (or DM me) by
> **Wednesday**. Keep it kind, and something she'd be happy for everyone to read.

**Family (WhatsApp):**
> Dada turns 80 on the 30th! 🎉 We're making him a card. Send me one memory
> of him and one thing he taught you, by Sunday. Voice notes are welcome too;
> I'll write them down. Kids can send a drawing!

**Class or club:**
> Coach Reyes is retiring after 22 seasons. What's one thing she said or did
> that you still remember? 1–3 sentences by Friday.

Ask for it to be sent **privately** to the organizer when the honoree is in
the same group chat, so the card stays a surprise.

## Reminders

One gentle reminder a day or two before the deadline is enough:
> Last call for Meena's card: one memory and what she meant to you, by tomorrow.

Never chase individuals publicly or list who hasn't replied.

## Tricky messages

| Message | What to do |
|---|---|
| "Good luck!!" | Fine to include; or ask, "Is there a moment you'd add?" |
| An inside joke others won't get | Ask the contributor if the honoree will love it and nobody's left out |
| A joke about age, looks, health, weight or personal life | Suggest a warmer version for them to approve |
| Private details (salary, a health issue, why they're leaving) | Ask them to remove it |
| Much longer than others | Ask if it's OK to trim, or keep it whole at the end |
| Different language | Keep it as written, add a translation underneath |
| Someone wants to stay anonymous | Credit as "Anonymous" (still checked) |

## After the deadline

Late messages: include them if the card hasn't gone out. Otherwise, offer to
send them separately, or add them to a second page.
