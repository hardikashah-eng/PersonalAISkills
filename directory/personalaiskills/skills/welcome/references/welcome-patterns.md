# Welcome patterns

## The personal note

1. **Welcome, and why we're glad**: "Welcome to the Platform team, Kenji. We
   were excited to hear about your work on the open-source scheduler, because
   that's exactly the problem we're about to take on."
2. **The first week**: three or four lines, not a schedule dump.
3. **Your buddy**: name, what they'll help with, how to reach them.
4. **Permission**: "No question is too small, and nobody expects you to ship
   anything this week."
5. **A warm close**: "We're really glad you're here."

## The team introduction (with their OK)

> Please welcome Kenji, who joins the Platform team today from Osaka (on
> Pacific time for now). Kenji has worked on open-source job schedulers and
> will start on our build queue. Fun fact he asked us to share: he has
> climbed Mount Fuji three times. Say hi, and invite him to your favourite
> lunch spot.

## A first-week outline

| Day | Shape |
|---|---|
| 1 | Laptop and accounts, lunch with the buddy, meet the manager |
| 2 | Architecture walkthrough, read the team's key docs |
| 3 | Pair with the buddy on a real (small) change |
| 4 | Ship a starter task: a doc fix, a small bug, a test |
| 5 | Week-one check-in: what's confusing, what's going well |

## Avoid

- "We're a family", "work hard, play hard", "hit the ground running".
- Quizzing or listing everything they must learn.
- Sharing anything from interviews, offers, previous pay, or why they left.
- Personal assumptions: about family, age, background, faith or drinking at events.
