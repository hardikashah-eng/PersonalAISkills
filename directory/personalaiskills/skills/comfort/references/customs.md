# Mourning customs and words: check before using

Customs vary widely between families, regions and levels of observance. Use
this as a prompt for what to ask the user, not as a rulebook. When unsure,
leave religious phrases out and say something universal ("I'm so sorry.
Thinking of you all.").

| Tradition | Things to know | Words people often use (only if it's their faith) |
|---|---|---|
| Hindu | A mourning period of about 13 days is common, ending in a ceremony; families often don't cook, so others bring simple vegetarian food | "Om Shanti" · "May his soul rest in peace" |
| Jewish | Shiva, about seven days of mourning at home; bringing a meal to the shiva house is customary; flowers are generally not | "May her memory be a blessing" · "Baruch dayan ha'emet" (often said by mourners) |
| Muslim | Burial is usually quick; about three days of mourning for the community; bring food to the family | "Inna lillahi wa inna ilayhi raji'un" · "May Allah grant him Jannah" |
| Christian | Wake or visitation, funeral, sometimes a meal afterwards; flowers and cards are common | "Rest in peace" · "Praying for you" (only if the user knows they'd welcome it) |
| Sikh | Prayers (Sehaj Paath) over about ten days; simple food at the gurdwara or home | "Waheguru" (by Sikh families) · "May he rest in peace" |
| Buddhist | Varies by country; memorial rites at certain intervals (for example, 49 days in some traditions) | "May she be at peace" |
| Chinese | White and dark colours for mourning, not red; avoid gifts in sets of four | "节哀顺变" (please restrain your grief) |
| Secular or unknown | Keep it universal | "I'm so sorry." · "Thinking of you." |

## Colours and gifts for cards and sketches

- In many East Asian cultures, white and chrysanthemums are tied to mourning.
  Fine for condolence, but never for celebrations.
- Red is festive in Chinese culture, so avoid it in condolence cards for
  Chinese families.
- Flowers are not customary for Jewish or Muslim mourning; food or a donation
  in the person's memory is.

Always defer to the user: they know the family.
