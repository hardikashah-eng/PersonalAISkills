# What to say, and what not to

## Phrases that help

| Purpose | Examples |
|---|---|
| Acknowledge | "I just heard, and I'm so sorry." · "There are no right words. I'm thinking of you." · "That's a hard thing to go through." |
| Name the person who died (if known) | "I'm so sorry about your dad." Using the name is a kindness: "Raj was one of a kind." |
| Share a memory | "I'll always remember how he…" · "Your mom made everyone feel like family at your graduation." |
| Affirm them | "You were such a good daughter to him." · "You've handled this with so much grace." |
| Offer something specific | "I'm bringing dinner Thursday; I'll leave it at the door." · "I'll walk Bruno this week." · "I'll take your on-call shift Friday." |
| Remove pressure | "No need to reply." · "I'll check in again next week, no pressure." |
| Close | "Thinking of you." · "Holding you close." · "With love." |

## Phrases that hurt (and the safety check blocks)

| Avoid | Why | Instead |
|---|---|---|
| "At least…" (they lived a long life, you can have another, you have savings) | Minimises the loss | Acknowledge it plainly |
| "Everything happens for a reason." / "It was meant to be." | Asks them to accept the pain as good | "I'm so sorry this happened." |
| "They're in a better place." | Assumes their beliefs | Only the family's own words, if the user asks |
| "I know exactly how you feel." / "When my…" | Shifts focus to the sender | "I can't imagine how hard this is." |
| "Stay strong." / "Don't cry." | Asks them to hide grief | "However you're feeling is okay." |
| "Let me know if you need anything." | Puts the work on them | One specific offer |
| "Time heals." / "Move on." | Rushes them | "I'm here for the long haul." |
| "You should… / Have you tried…?" | Advice they didn't ask for | Leave it out |

## By situation

- **Death of a loved one:** acknowledge, use the name, share a memory, a
  specific offer, no need to reply. Follow their mourning customs
  (`customs.md`).
- **Pet loss:** treat it as real grief; use the pet's name; a memory helps
  ("Bruno's tail-wag greetings at the door").
- **Illness or surgery (theirs or a loved one's):** don't ask for or mention
  details; no prognoses; offer practical help (meals, rides, errands); "No need
  to update me."
- **Pregnancy loss:** very short, very gentle; "I'm so sorry. I'm here."; no
  "at least", no "next time", no humour, no food talk unless offering a meal.
- **Layoff or job loss:** no "it's a blessing in disguise"; affirm them
  ("Any team would be lucky to have you"); a concrete offer (an introduction,
  reviewing a CV, coffee) only if appropriate; humour sometimes welcome.
- **Breakup or divorce:** no judgement of the ex; affirm them; a plan
  ("Dinner at ours on Friday?").
- **A hard anniversary:** "Thinking of you and your mom today" can mean more
  than anything else; the exact day matters.
- **Disaster (fire, flood):** practical first: a place to stay, supplies,
  childcare; keep words short.

## Length

Text or chat: 25–60 words. Card or email: 60–120 words. Shorter is kinder.
