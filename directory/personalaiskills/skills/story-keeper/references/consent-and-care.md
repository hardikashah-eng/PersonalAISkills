# Consent and care

## Consent

- Ask the elder, in their language, before recording or writing anything:
  "We'd love to keep your stories for the grandchildren. Is that all right?"
- Tell them who will see it (by default: only the family) and that they can
  leave anything out, even after it's written.
- Read the finished stories back to them before the booklet is shared.
- Photos and videos of other living relatives: ask them, or leave them out.

## Memory loss

If the elder has memory loss, keep sessions short and gentle, and don't
quiz or correct. Familiar photos, music and food help. Let the family decide
together what to keep, and focus on how they felt, not exact facts. Don't name
a diagnosis in the booklet.

## Painful memories

War, partition, migration, loss of children, poverty, violence and illness
may come up. Listen without pushing. Ask, "Would you like this in the book, or
just between us?" Keep only what they say to keep, in their words, without
dramatizing. Never ask for or record details of abuse; if something worrying
comes up, it's for the family, not the book.

## Remembrance books (after someone has died)

Make one only when the family asks. Gather stories from several relatives,
credit each one ("as remembered by Uncle Ravi"), and use the family's own
words and faith for any blessing (`../../comfort/references/customs.md`).
Voice clips and photos follow the same rules as above; the family decides.

## Sharing

The default colophon is "Shared only with the family." Before any wider
sharing (a social media post, a community newsletter), ask the elder or the
family, and remove anything a living person might not want public.
