# Interview guide

Pick 10–15 questions, not all of them. Ask in the elder's language. Start
light, follow their energy, and let one good story run long rather than racing
through the list.

## Before you start

- Sit somewhere comfortable, with tea. Mid-morning is often best.
- Ask: "Is it all right if I record this, just for the family?" Respect a no.
- Bring a photo or an object (an old letter, a utensil, a wedding photo).
  Objects unlock memories that questions don't.
- 30–45 minutes per session. Stop while it's still fun.

## How to listen

- Follow up with "What happened next?", "What did it look like?", "What did
  that smell like?", "What did you say?". Details make a story.
- Let silences sit. The best answers often come after a pause.
- Don't correct their dates or versions. Note them; the family can compare later.
- If a memory turns painful, slow down: "We don't have to talk about this."
  Move on unless they want to continue (`consent-and-care.md`).

## Questions by chapter

**Childhood**
- What's the first home you remember? Walk me through it, room by room.
- What games did you play? Who was your best friend?
- What did your mother cook that you still miss?
- What were you scolded for most?

**Family and the people before you**
- What do you remember about your grandparents?
- What were your parents like when you were small? What did they teach you?
- Is there a family saying, joke or story everyone knows?

**School and work**
- What did you want to be when you grew up?
- What was your first job? What was your first pay spent on? (Leave out amounts.)
- Who believed in you when it mattered?

**Love and marriage**
- How did you meet? What did you think the first time you saw each other?
- What was your wedding day like? What went wrong that you laugh about now?

**Moving and big changes**
- Did you ever move far from home? What did you take with you?
- What was hardest about starting over, and who helped?

**Traditions, festivals and food**
- How did your family celebrate Diwali / Eid / Christmas / New Year?
- Which recipe should never be lost? Can you show me how you make it?

**Looking back**
- What are you proudest of?
- What's changed most since you were young? What do you wish hadn't changed?
- What made you laugh the hardest in your life?

**For the grandchildren**
- What do you want them to know about where they come from?
- What's one piece of advice you'd give them?
- Is there anything you've never told anyone that you'd like the family to know?
  (Only keep it if they say so.)

## Afterwards

- Write it up within a day, while it's fresh, in their words.
- Read it back to them. Ask: "Did I get that right? Is there anything you'd
  like to change or leave out?"
