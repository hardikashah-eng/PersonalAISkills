# Thanking family (`--family`)

Family notes are the most personal and often the hardest to write, because the
gratitude is decades deep and the words feel too small. Keep them simple,
specific, and in the family's own voice.

`--family [name]` is for one relative ("--family Mom"), and `--family` alone is
for the whole family or a household ("to all of you"). It also works from plain
language ("a Thanksgiving note to my parents").

## 1. Gather

- **Who**, and what the sender calls them: Mom, Amma, Ba, Papa, Nani, Abuela,
  Grandpa Joe. Use exactly that form throughout.
- **The relationship**: parent, grandparent, sibling, spouse/partner, child,
  in-law, aunt or uncle, cousin, chosen family.
- **The language of the home.** Family notes are where the native language
  matters most; a whole note, or the greeting, one key line and the blessing
  in the heart language, can mean more than anything else in it.
- **Anything tender right now** (a loss, illness, a strained relationship,
  a divorce). Ask gently once; steer around it silently.

## 2. Find the moment

Family gratitude is often about the long arc ("everything you did for me").
Turn the arc into **one picture**:
- not "thank you for all your sacrifices", but "the way you waited up with tea
  every night I studied for finals";
- not "thank you for always being there", but "the Saturday you drove three
  hours just to help me move one bookshelf".

Sources: the user's memory first (ask: "Is there one moment that sums it up?"),
then family chat threads (WhatsApp/SMS only with permission), shared photo
albums, and old emails. Family group chats are only read if the user points to
one.

## 3. Wording that fits family

| To | Tone | Address and sign-off |
|---|---|---|
| Parents | reverent, warm, grateful for the long arc | "Dear Mom and Dad," … "Your loving son," |
| Grandparents / elders | respectful, gentle, simple; blessings welcome | "Dear Nani," / "Respected Dada-ji," … "With love and respect," |
| Siblings | affectionate, lightly playful, never teasing | nickname … "Love, your big sister," |
| Spouse / partner | tender, private; keep it tasteful if others may see | pet name only if the user uses it … "Always," |
| Children (thanking them) | proud, encouraging, never pressuring | "My dear Sofía," … "Love, Papa" |
| In-laws | warm, gracious, slightly formal | as the user addresses them |
| The whole family | inclusive; the family as a "we" | "To all of you," / "Dear family," |

## 4. Family rules

1. **No ranking or comparison**: never "my favourite sibling", "unlike your
   brother", or anything that weighs one relative against another.
2. **Whole-family notes include everyone** in the household or the group the
   user names, especially in-laws, step-family, and the youngest and oldest.
   If naming people, name everyone (check against the family list).
3. **No guilt or debt language**: avoid "after all you sacrificed", "I can
   never repay you", "I know I don't call enough". Gratitude, not obligation.
4. **No pressure**: no "when are you…" or hints about marriage, children,
   careers, or health.
5. **Respect blended and chosen families.** Use the terms the user uses;
   don't assume two parents, a mother and a father, or biological ties.
6. **Remembrance only on request.** A relative who has passed is mentioned
   only if the user asks, and then gently ("Dad would have loved this").
7. **Blessings and religion** follow the family's tradition and the user's
   lead ("May Lakshmi bless our home", "God bless", or none).
8. **Private matters stay private**: health, money, and family conflict stay
   out even if the note will only be read at home.

## 5. Photo and art

- **Photo:** a family photo from a happy time (a holiday table, a trip, a
  milestone, a candid laugh). Children in the photo only if they're the user's
  own family and the user chooses it. Respect elders' or relatives' known
  wishes about photos. Avoid photos with someone who has passed or an ex-partner
  unless the user specifically wants them. Follow `photos.md`.
- **Art motifs:** a family tree with one leaf per person, a home with lit
  windows, a long table, a rangoli or wreath for the festival, a path walked
  together, hands around a cup.

## 6. Structure (family)

1. Quote (optional for family; a proverb or blessing in the home language often
   fits better).
2. Greeting in the family's form of address.
3. The one moment.
4. What it gave the sender (who they became, what they carry).
5. Paying it forward (often the most moving part in a family note, see
   `paying-it-forward.md`).
6. Festival blessing or occasion line.
7. Close in the family's way.
