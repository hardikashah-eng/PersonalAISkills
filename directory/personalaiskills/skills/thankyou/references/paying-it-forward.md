# Paying it forward

The deepest thank-you shows that the gift didn't stop with the sender. "You
taught me to read the error slowly, and now I teach every new engineer the
same" tells the giver their kindness is still travelling. It is optional and
used only when it is true.

## When to include it

- The sender genuinely passes on what they received: a habit, a skill, a
  kindness, a value, or an opportunity they now open for others.
- The user confirms it. Ask once: "Is there a way you've passed this on, such
  as teaching it, doing the same for someone else, or building on it?"
- Skip it if there's nothing real, or if it would make the note about the sender.

## Where to find it

- **The user's own words**: the best source by far. Ask.
- **Their own activity**, read lightly: recurring mentoring 1:1s on their
  calendar, a talk or workshop they gave, a team practice they started,
  volunteering. Count and describe; don't quote other people's messages.
- **Team/org notes**: what the shared achievement now enables for others
  ("three other teams now build on it"), or how the sender is carrying the
  team's way of working into new places.
- **Family notes**: what the sender now does for their own children, siblings,
  parents, or community ("Now I'm the one at the kitchen table with Sofía's
  homework, the way you sat with me").

## How to write it

- **One or two sentences**, placed after "what it says about them" and before
  the occasion line.
- **Credit flows back to the giver**: frame it as their influence ("your
  patience lives on in how I…"), not as the sender's achievement.
- **Modest and concrete**: "every new engineer I've mentored since", not
  "hundreds of people now benefit from my leadership".
- **Protect the people helped**: no names, details, or stories about the
  people the sender helped, unless they're family members the user names and
  it's appropriate. "A few new engineers", "my daughter", or "our interns"
  is enough.
- **No numbers that sound like metrics** unless small and human ("three
  interns this summer").
- **Never invent it.**

## Examples

| Note | Paying-it-forward line |
|---|---|
| Mentor | "I've passed that on to every engineer I've mentored since." |
| Teacher | "I volunteer at a coding club now, and I hear your voice every time a student is stuck." |
| Team (from leader) | "I'm taking the way you worked, no one debugs alone, into every team I join." |
| Team (impact) | "Three other teams now run their tests on what you built." |
| Organization | "Last spring I started mentoring at the same weekend workshop that once took a chance on me." |
| Parents | "Now I'm the one at the kitchen table with Sofía's homework, the way you sat with me." |
| Friend | "When a friend of mine lost her job this year, I did exactly what you did for me: I just showed up." |
