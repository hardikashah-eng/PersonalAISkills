# Thanking a team or an organization (`--team`, `--org`)

A note to a group should do what a note to one person does: prove the sender
noticed. Here that means naming the goal you reached **together**, what it took
from them, and what it made possible.

- `--team [name]`: a group the sender worked *with* or led, such as their own
  team, a project crew, a cross-team squad, a class, or a volunteer group.
- `--org [name]`: an organization as a whole, such as a partner company, a
  nonprofit, a school, a hospital staff, a club, a former employer, or a
  community group.

Both modes also work from plain language ("thank my team for the launch",
"a thank-you to the robotics club"); the flags just make it explicit.

## 1. Gather

Ask only what isn't already clear:
- **Who exactly is "the team"?** Name or description, and roughly who's in it.
  For a team note that names people, get the full roster (see rule 3 below).
- **What did you achieve together?** The goal, the moment it landed, and why
  it mattered.
- **Sender's role:** leader, peer, partner, customer, or beneficiary. This sets
  the tone (see below).
- **Where it goes:** team channel post, all-hands slide, email to the group,
  or a printed card for the office. Default: a card plus a channel-ready text.

## 2. Find the shared achievement

Search the connectors for the story of the goal, not for individual praise:
- **Chat** (Slack, Teams, Lark): launch or "we did it" messages in the team
  channel, milestone threads, celebration emoji storms 🎉.
- **Email:** release notes, milestone announcements, partner thank-yous,
  customer feedback forwarded to the team.
- **Calendar:** the offsite, the launch day, the long run of war-room meetings
  (span and count, not content).
- **Docs / trackers:** the plan that set the goal, and the retro that closed it.

Build the arc: **the goal → what it took → the moment it landed → what it made
possible.** Show it to the user as 2–3 one-line candidates and let them pick.

> 1. Oct 2026 — the simulation farm migration shipped: test runs that took a week now finish the same afternoon.
> 2. Mar 2026 — the team held the line through a tough release while two people were out.

## 3. Rules for group notes

1. **Credit the group first.** Lead with the shared goal and the "we".
2. **Name everyone or no one.** If you call out individuals, include every
   person on the roster, each with something specific. One forgotten name
   hurts more than the note helps. When in doubt, name no one. With a roster,
   Check the note against the roster: if anyone is named, everyone must be.
3. **Include everyone who made it happen**, including remote teammates,
   contractors, interns, people on leave, and those who moved on mid-project.
   Prefer "everyone who touched this" to a list that might miss someone.
4. **No confidential details** if the note might travel: no revenue, costs,
   headcount, unreleased product names, customer names, or internal metrics.
   Describe impact in human terms ("a week of waiting became an afternoon").
   Never mention ratings, promotions, performance reviews, or layoffs.
5. **No ranking or comparison** between people or with other teams.
6. **Inclusive language:** "everyone", "team", "folks", not "guys"; no
   "rockstars" or "ninjas".
7. **Not a performance review.** A leader's thank-you should feel like
   gratitude, not evaluation ("exceeded expectations" belongs elsewhere).
8. **Speaking for an organization:** if the sender writes *on behalf of* their
   company, keep to what they're comfortable saying publicly, and don't make
   commitments or claims for the company.

## 4. Tone by the sender's role

| Sender is… | Tone | Opening |
|---|---|---|
| Leader of the team | warm, proud, humble; credit up, not to self | "To the Platform team," |
| Peer / teammate | collegial, shared pride | "Team," / "Hi all," |
| Partner / another team | appreciative, specific about the collaboration | "To everyone on the Data Infra team," |
| Customer / beneficiary of an org | sincere, concrete about the difference | "Dear friends at Harbor Community Robotics," |
| Former employee to an org | gracious, forward-looking, no score-settling | "To everyone at …," |

## 5. Structure (team / org)

1. **Quote**: teamwork or shared effort (Helen Keller's "Alone we can do so
   little; together we can do so much"; "Proverb" if attribution is uncertain).
2. **Greeting** to the group.
3. **The goal and the moment**: what you set out to do and the moment it landed.
4. **What it took**: the effort, care, or grit, described collectively (or for
   every named person, if naming).
5. **What it made possible**: for the sender, for users, for other teams.
6. **Paying it forward** (optional, see `paying-it-forward.md`).
7. **Occasion tie-in**, then a warm close and signature.

## 6. Photo and art

- **Photo:** a team or org photo from the moment, such as the launch-day group
  shot, the offsite, or the event. Prefer photos already shared with that group
  (team channel, offsite album). Everyone pictured should belong to the group,
  and a photo where a few people are missing is better replaced by a photo of
  the shared thing (the launch cake, the finished build, the event banner).
  Follow `photos.md`, plus: no one who has asked not to be photographed, no
  screens or badges with names, and crop out unrelated passers-by.
- **Art motifs:** a rowing boat with one oar per person, a relay baton, a
  constellation with one star per team member, interlocking gears, a bridge
  built from many planks, a summit flag. Never draw logos.

## 7. Delivery

- **Team channel:** a channel-ready version (no greeting-card formatting, about
  80–150 words), with the photo to attach. Draft only; the user posts it.
- **Card:** the rendered card for printing or sharing in the team space.
- **Email to the group:** a draft with the clean photo attached, never sent.
