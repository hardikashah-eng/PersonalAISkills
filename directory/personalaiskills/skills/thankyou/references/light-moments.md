# A light moment: a link to something that makes them smile

Used by Comfort and Tribute (and optionally ThankYou and Reconnect). A short,
well-chosen comedy scene can say "I know you, and I'm still here" better than
more words, if, and only if, the person would welcome it.

## When to use one

- **The user says the person would enjoy it** and names or approves the show
  or scene ("she quotes Seinfeld constantly"). Never assume someone's taste.
- **Tribute:** often. Farewells, retirements and celebrations are a natural fit.
- **Comfort:** only for lighter hard times (a rough week, a layoff, a move, a
  minor surgery recovery, a breakup) and only with the user's go-ahead. **Never**
  for a fresh loss, a serious diagnosis, pregnancy loss, or anything violent or
  traumatic. Some weeks later, "something to make you laugh when you're ready"
  can be right, if the user knows the person well.
- At most one per note, offered as an aside: "For a smile, whenever you want
  one:". Never as the point of the message.

## Where the link comes from

Sitcoms such as *Seinfeld*, *Friends*, *The Big Bang Theory* or *The Office*
are **copyrighted, not public domain**. So:

1. **Link, never copy.** Use the link to the scene on an official source: the
   show's, studio's or network's own channel, or the official streaming
   service. Never download, re-host, embed copied files, or attach a clip.
2. **Find and check it at the time of use.** Search for the official clip,
   open the page to confirm it is the right scene and comes from the
   rights-holder's own channel, and record it in `note.json`:
   `"light_moment": {"title": "…", "url": "…", "source": "official channel name"}`.
   If no official clip can be confirmed, name the episode instead ("Seinfeld,
   'The Serenity Now', season 9") and let the user find it on their
   streaming service, or use a public-domain option below.
3. **No long quotes or lyrics.** A catchphrase of two or three words is fine
   ("Serenity now!", "Pivot!"); dialogue passages and song lyrics are not.
4. **Check the scene fits.** Nothing that mocks illness, death, bodies, grief,
   or the very thing the person is going through, and no stereotypes.

### Truly public-domain comedy (free to link and share)

Films published in the US before 1929 are in the public domain there. Silent
comedy shorts are wordless, so they work in any language. They're on
Wikimedia Commons and the Internet Archive, for example:

- **Buster Keaton, *One Week* (1920):** newlyweds build a kit house that goes
  spectacularly wrong. Good for a move, a new chapter, or a farewell.
  Wikimedia Commons: `https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:One_Week_(1920_film)`
- Other Keaton, Chaplin and Harold Lloyd shorts from before 1929: confirm the
  public-domain status and the link on the file's Commons or Internet Archive
  page before using one.

## Scene ideas by mood (find the official link when used)

| Mood | Scene | Show (season, episode) |
|---|---|---|
| Stress, a rough week, a layoff | Frank Costanza's "Serenity now!" | *Seinfeld*, "The Serenity Now" (S9 E3, 1997) |
| Moving, a new home, a big change | Ross shouting "Pivot!" at a sofa on the stairs | *Friends*, "The One with the Cop" (S5 E16) |
| Under the weather, recovering | Sheldon asking for "Soft Kitty" when he's sick (link only; never quote the song) | *The Big Bang Theory* |
| A farewell, a team wrap-up | A favourite team-moment scene the user names | the user's choice |

Confirm the season and episode with a quick search before printing them.

## How it appears

- **Card:** a small line after the closing: "For a smile, whenever you want
  one: *One Week* (Buster Keaton, 1920)". The link is clickable in the HTML
  card; the image version shows the title only.
- **Message text:** the same line followed by the URL, so WhatsApp, Slack and
  email show a preview.
