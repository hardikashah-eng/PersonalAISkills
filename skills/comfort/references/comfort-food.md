# A food thought

Food is one of the oldest ways people care for each other in hard times. In
the note, a food thought is a gentle idea, not instructions. Usually it's
something the sender will **bring**; from afar, it's permission for the
person to keep things simple.

## How to phrase it

- **Bringing:** "I'm making a pot of dal on Thursday and I'll leave some at
  your door. No need to come out." / "Soup's on its way Saturday."
- **From afar:** "If cooking feels like too much this week, a bowl of
  something warm counts as dinner." / "I've sent you a little something for
  takeaway nights." (Mention a gift card only if the user is actually sending
  one.)
- **A shared memory:** "Next time you're up for it, chai at our old spot is on me."
- Keep it to one line. Never tell them what they should eat.

## Warm, simple ideas by tradition (when the user knows theirs)

| Often welcome | Examples |
|---|---|
| South Asian homes | khichdi, dal and rice, a thermos of chai, fruit |
| East Asian homes | congee (jook), noodle soup, steamed buns, fruit |
| Jewish homes | a meal for the shiva house, bagels, soup, baked goods |
| Latin American homes | caldo or sopa, arroz con pollo, pan dulce |
| Middle Eastern homes | lentil soup, rice dishes, dates |
| Many homes | chicken or vegetable soup, a casserole or lasagna, bread, fruit, breakfast for the next morning |

These are ideas, not rules. Follow what the family eats, not assumptions.

## When to be careful, or skip food

- **Dietary and faith rules:** vegetarian, vegan, halal, kosher, Jain, fasting
  periods, allergies. If unsure, suggest a fruit basket, or ask a close
  friend of the family, or skip food.
- **Mourning customs:** in some traditions the grieving family doesn't cook
  for a period and others bring food; in others there are restrictions on
  what's eaten. Check `customs.md`, and when in doubt, offer rather than
  deliver.
- **Illness, treatment or recovery:** appetite and diet may be affected; offer
  help ("I'm happy to bring anything that sounds good, or nothing at all")
  rather than naming a dish.
- **Pregnancy loss, or any sign of a difficult relationship with food:** leave
  food out unless offering a meal drop-off with no details.
- **Never alcohol** as a comfort suggestion.

## For the card

A food thought can inspire the sketch: a steaming bowl, a thermos and two cups,
a loaf of bread wrapped in cloth. Keep it simple and warm.
