# Hooks: the reason to say hello now

A good hook makes the message feel natural, specific, and easy to answer.
Use one. It must be true.

## Hook types (in order of preference)

### 1. Shared memory
Something the two of them lived through together.
- "Walked past a food truck today with a line around the block and immediately
  thought of our 'it's worth the wait' theory from the Austin offsite."
- "Found the whiteboard photo from the night we finally got the demo working."
Best when it's small, specific, and happy. Skip memories tied to a hard time
unless the user wants that.

### 2. "Thought of you" trigger
Something real in the user's current life that connects to the person.
- "I just used your 'read the error slowly' line on a new engineer and realised I never told you how often I quote you."
- "Started running again — you were the one who got me to my first 10K."
Ask the user for one if you don't have it. Never invent.

### 3. Their public milestone
Only what they shared with their network (a LinkedIn post, a talk, a launch).
- "Saw you're leading the new robotics group — that's so you. Congratulations!"
Congratulate; don't analyse, don't ask for details they didn't share.

### 4. Season or festival
A natural low-pressure opener — "Happy Diwali", "新年快乐", "Eid Mubarak",
"Happy Thanksgiving" — only for a holiday they celebrate. Pair with one warm,
specific line so it isn't a broadcast.

### 5. Genuine question
Something they'd enjoy answering.
- "Still the best person I know for book recommendations — what's the one I should read this winter?"

## Making the door easy

End with **one** light question or a soft invitation:
- "What are you building these days?"
- "Would love to catch up properly sometime if you're up for it."
- "No need for a long reply — just wanted you to know."

Avoid stacked questions, big asks, or anything that needs a calendar.

## Phrases to avoid

| Avoid | Why | Instead |
|---|---|---|
| "It's been forever!" / "Long time no talk!" / "3 years already!" | counts the gap, invites guilt | open with the hook |
| "Sorry I disappeared" / "I'm the worst at staying in touch" | makes them reassure you | skip it; warmth says it |
| "We should catch up sometime" (alone) | vague, easy to ignore | one specific, light question |
| "I saw on your profile that you moved to…" / "noticed you were at…" | feels like monitoring | only reference what they shared *with you* or announced publicly |
| "Quick favour…" / "Could you refer me…" in a catch-up | hidden agenda | reconnect first; ask later, openly |
| "How's your health / your divorce / the job hunt?" | sensitive, presumptuous | let them share what they want |
| "You probably don't remember me" | self-deprecating, awkward | name the shared context plainly |

## Channel fit

- WhatsApp / SMS: 25–60 words, casual, a single emoji is fine if that was their style.
- Slack / Teams: short, friendly, no formatting.
- LinkedIn: 50–100 words, warm-professional; mention the shared context in the first line.
- Email: 50–100 words; subject line short and human ("Thought of you today", "Happy Diwali, Mei!").
