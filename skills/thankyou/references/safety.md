# Safety and kindness

A thank-you note may be read by others (a card on a desk, a forwarded email,
a family table). Write it so that it is safe for anyone to see.

## Never include

**Personally identifiable information**: home or work addresses, phone numbers,
email addresses, social handles, ID / passport / licence / account numbers,
birthdates, ages of children, licence plates, exact locations, internal project
codenames, company-confidential details, names of third parties who did not
consent.

**Financial information**: amounts of money, salaries, offers, raises, loans,
debts, gifts with a price, bank or card references, investment details.

**Health information**: illnesses, diagnoses, injuries, surgeries, pregnancy,
fertility, mental health, therapy, medications, hospitals, disabilities, addiction,
recovery, a family member's health.

**Other sensitive areas**: legal trouble, immigration, religion or politics
(unless the user explicitly frames the note that way), relationship break-ups,
layoffs/firings of others, anything the recipient told the user in confidence.

## How to abstract — keep the feeling, drop the detail

| Raw moment | Write instead |
|---|---|
| "covered my rent when I was broke" | "stepped in, no questions asked, when I needed it most" |
| "visited me at Scripps after my surgery" | "showed up for me during a really hard stretch" |
| "helped me negotiate a $40k higher offer" | "helped me find the confidence to ask for what I was worth" |
| "talked me through my panic attacks" | "were a steady, calm voice when I needed one" |
| "drove my daughter to school for a month" | "made a hard month so much easier for our family" |
| "introduced me to Jane Doe at Acme for the L7 role" | "made the introduction that opened a new chapter for me" |
| "fixed the outage on PROJ-4412 at 2am" | "jumped in late at night when everything was on fire" |

## Kindness rules

- No sarcasm, teasing, or backhanded compliments, even if the relationship is
  jokey — written humour lands differently.
- No judgement of the recipient or anyone else; no comparisons.
- No guilt ("I know I never thank you enough…" is fine once; never "you probably don't remember").
- Nothing explicit, crude, flirtatious, or romantic unless the user and
  recipient are partners and the user asks for that tone — and even then keep it tasteful.
- Don't overstate ("you saved my life") unless the user says it literally and wants it.
- Avoid assumptions about the recipient's beliefs, family, or holiday traditions;
  "Happy Thanksgiving" only if the user indicates they celebrate it.
- If the user asks for a note that would hurt, shame, or pressure the recipient,
  gently offer a kinder version instead.
