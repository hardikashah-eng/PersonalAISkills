# Repair patterns

## By situation

| Situation | Own it | Make it right |
|---|---|---|
| Cut someone off or dismissed an idea | "I cut you off while you were explaining X." | Give them the floor next time; put their point in the notes |
| Sharp comment in a review or chat | "My comment on your PR was harsher than it needed to be." | Edit or follow up on the thread with the useful part, kindly |
| Missed commitment | "I said I'd review your design by Friday, and I didn't." | A new date you'll keep, or hand it to someone who can |
| Credit not given | "In the demo I said 'we' built the exporter. You built it." | Correct it in the same forum, by name |
| Dropped handoff or broke something | "I merged without telling on-call, and you got paged at 2 a.m." | Fix the process, and offer to take the next shift |
| A tense debate (both sides) | A follow-up, not an apology: "Today got heated. I value how much you care about this." | Propose a calmer next step: a doc, a smaller group |

## Avoid

- "I'm sorry if you felt…", "sorry you feel that way", "mistakes were made"
- "I'm sorry, but…" or any excuse in the same breath
- Over-apologising, self-criticism, or asking them to reassure you
- Asking for forgiveness ("I hope you can forgive me")
- Apologising in public for something private, or only in private for something public
- Promises you can't keep

## Personal life

The same shape works with a partner, a friend or family, by text or in
person: what I did, the effect on you, I'm sorry, what I'll do differently.
Keep it shorter, and say it in person when you can.
