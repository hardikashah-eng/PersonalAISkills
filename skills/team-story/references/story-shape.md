# The shape of a team story

## The arc

1. **Headline** (one sentence): the outcome, in the team's own terms. "Builds
   that used to queue for twenty minutes now start in under two."
2. **Chapters** (three to five): find the problem → build the fix → roll it out
   → what's next. Each with when, what happened, and who did what.
3. **In their words**: two to four short quotes.
4. **Thank you to**: people outside the team who made it possible.
5. **Closing**: one warm line.

## Good contributor lines

Name the action and the effect, like a Kudos note in miniature:

- "found the lockstep retries and shipped jittered backoff with a load test"
- "fixed the flaky queue test in his first week, so rollouts stopped being retried by hand"
- "reviewed every tiling-related change and kept the compiler team in the loop"

Avoid: "helped out", "was instrumental", "crushed it", "rockstar".

## Who to include

- Everyone who shipped, reviewed, tested, documented, coordinated, was on call
  or unblocked others during the period.
- New and junior people, for real contributions. Their first wins matter.
- Other teams and leaders who helped, in `also_thanks`.
- Ask the user, "Anyone we're missing?" before it goes anywhere.

## For an all-hands

One slide per chapter, the map on its own slide, quotes on one slide, and a
thank-you slide. Let team members present their own chapter if they'd like to.
