# Introduction patterns

| Kind | The reason to name | Next step |
|---|---|---|
| New hire meets an expert | what the expert built, and what the new hire is about to work on | a 20-minute walkthrough |
| Same problem, different teams | the overlap and what each has learned | share docs, then a call if useful |
| Mentor and mentee | what the mentee wants to grow, and why this mentor | one coffee to see if it fits |
| Job seeker and hiring manager | only what you can vouch for, first-hand | the seeker sends a CV; the manager decides |
| Customer or partner | the business reason, with your company's OK | the owner of the relationship leads |
| Personal (a friend new in town, a fellow parent) | the shared interest or situation | a coffee or a walk |

## Asks that work

- Name the other person and the specific reason.
- Say what it involves (20 minutes, an email, a doc).
- Give an easy out: "Totally fine to say no."
- Don't forward the other person's message or details yet.

## Intros that work

- Short: both should read it in under a minute.
- Equal: each gets a warm one-line description.
- One next step, and the introducer steps back.

## Avoid

- Introducing without asking ("I've cc'd you both!").
- "You two should chat" with no reason.
- Asking the senior person to "help out" someone, as if it's a favour owed.
- Sharing why someone's looking for a job, or anything personal.
